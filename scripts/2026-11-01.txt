Parashah: Chayei Sara (Genesis 23:1-25:18)
Date: 2026-11-01

Welcome to Parashah in Brief, and thanks so much for joining me this week. This week's Torah portion is called Chayei Sarah, which means "the life of Sarah," and it picks up the story of Abraham's family at a pivotal turning point — a time of loss, legacy, and new beginnings.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Genesis, chapter twenty-three, verse one, through chapter twenty-five, verse eighteen. The portion opens with the death of Sarah at the age of one hundred and twenty-seven in the town of Hebron, in the land of Canaan. Abraham mourns deeply for her and then sets out to find a proper burial place, negotiating with the local Hittites to purchase a plot of land. After a formal and somewhat elaborate exchange, he buys the cave of Machpelah and its surrounding field from a man named Ephron for four hundred shekels of silver — and there he lays Sarah to rest. With Sarah gone, Abraham turns his attention to the future: he sends his most trusted servant on a journey back to his homeland to find a wife for his son Isaac, making the servant swear that he will not choose a woman from among the Canaanites. The servant travels to the region of Aram-naharaim and stops at a well outside the city, where he prays for a sign — that the right young woman will offer water not only to him but also to his camels. Almost immediately, a beautiful and generous young woman named Rebekah appears and does exactly that, drawing water again and again for all ten camels. The servant is overjoyed and learns that she is from Abraham's own extended family. He goes to her household, tells the whole story of his mission, and her family agrees that this match has been guided by God. When asked directly whether she will go, Rebekah says simply, "I will." She travels back to Canaan, where Isaac sees her approaching in the field at evening, and he takes her as his wife, finding comfort after his mother's death. The portion then tells us that Abraham married again, had more children, and eventually died at the age of one hundred and seventy-five, full of years and contented. His sons Isaac and Ishmael come together to bury him in the cave of Machpelah, beside Sarah. The portion closes with a brief genealogy of Ishmael's twelve sons and their tribes.

Parashah: Chukat-Balak (Numbers 19:1-25:9)
Date: 2026-06-21

Welcome back to Parashah in Brief! This week we have a double portion called Chukat-Balak, and it's a big one. We're deep in the book of Numbers now, following the Israelites through the final stretch of their forty-year journey toward the Promised Land, and this portion is packed with drama, loss, and some truly unforgettable moments.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Numbers, chapter nineteen, verse one, through chapter twenty-five, verse nine. The portion opens with a mysterious ritual — God commands the Israelites to prepare a red cow, completely unblemished, which is burned to ashes and mixed with water to purify anyone who has come into contact with the dead. Then we leap forward to the fortieth year of wandering: the people arrive at Kadesh, and Miriam, Moses' sister, dies and is buried there. Immediately after, the community runs out of water and turns on Moses and Aaron. God tells Moses to speak to a rock to bring forth water, but in his frustration Moses strikes the rock twice with his staff instead. Water flows, but God tells Moses and Aaron that because they did not trust Him fully, neither of them will enter the Promised Land. Soon after, Aaron dies on Mount Hor, and his priestly garments are passed to his son Eleazar, while the whole nation mourns for thirty days. As the people continue their journey, they complain again, and God sends venomous serpents among them. When they repent, God tells Moses to craft a copper serpent and mount it on a pole — anyone bitten who looks at it is healed. Israel then wins dramatic military victories over King Sihon of the Amorites and King Og of Bashan, taking possession of their lands. This alarming news reaches Balak, the king of Moab, who sends for a famous prophet named Balaam to come and curse Israel. Balaam's journey becomes one of the Torah's most memorable scenes: his own donkey sees an angel of God blocking the road and refuses to move, and when Balaam beats her, God opens the donkey's mouth and she speaks, rebuking her master. Balaam's eyes are finally opened, he sees the angel, and he is warned that he may speak only what God commands.

Parashah: Shmini Atzeret (Deuteronomy 14:22-16:17)
Date: 2026-09-27

Welcome to Parashah in Brief! I'm so glad you're joining me this week. Today we're looking at the Torah portion read on Shmini Atzeret, the joyful eighth day of assembly that caps the fall festival season. This reading comes from the book of Deuteronomy, where Moses is delivering his final teachings to the Israelites before they enter the Promised Land.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Deuteronomy, chapter fourteen, verse twenty-two, through chapter sixteen, verse seventeen. Moses begins by commanding the people to set aside a tenth of their harvest every year — a tithe — and to consume it joyfully in the presence of God at the chosen place of worship. If the journey is too long, they may convert their tithe into money, travel to that holy place, and spend it on whatever their heart desires — cattle, wine, or anything else — and feast there with their household. But every third year, the full tithe stays local, so that the Levites, the strangers, the orphans, and the widows can come and eat their fill. Moses then turns to the sabbatical year, commanding that every seventh year all debts among fellow Israelites must be released. He urges the people never to harden their hearts against those in need, warning them not to hold back generosity just because the year of remission is near. "Give readily and have no regrets," he says, "for there will never cease to be needy ones in your land." He also teaches that Hebrew servants must go free after six years of service and must not be sent away empty-handed — they should be furnished generously, because the Israelites themselves were once slaves in Egypt and God redeemed them. Next, Moses addresses the firstborn of the herds and flocks, which must be consecrated to God. Finally, he lays out the three pilgrimage festivals: Passover, the Feast of Unleavened Bread, recalling the hurried departure from Egypt; the Feast of Weeks, celebrated seven weeks after the first harvest; and the Feast of Booths, held after the autumn ingathering. On each of these occasions, all males must appear before God at the chosen place, and no one shall come empty-handed — each person brings a gift according to the blessings they have received.

Parashah: Nitzavim-Vayeilech (Deuteronomy 29:9-31:30)
Date: 2026-08-30

Welcome to Parashah in Brief! I'm so glad you're here with me this week. We're looking at a powerful double portion called Nitzavim-Vayeilech, which takes us near the very end of Deuteronomy — and near the very end of Moses's life. These are among his final words to the Israelite people before they cross the Jordan River into the Promised Land.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Deuteronomy, chapter twenty-nine, verse nine, through chapter thirty-one, verse thirty. Moses gathers the entire nation — leaders, elders, men, women, children, even the woodchoppers and water drawers — and declares that they are all entering into a covenant with God. He makes clear this covenant is not only with those standing there that day, but also with every future generation yet to come. He warns them sternly: if anyone turns away to worship foreign gods, thinking they'll be safe, God's anger will fall upon them and upon the land itself, leaving it scorched like Sodom and Gomorrah. But then Moses offers a breathtaking promise of hope — even if the people are scattered to the very ends of the earth, if they return to God with all their heart, God will gather them back, restore their fortunes, and open up their hearts to love. Moses then delivers one of the most stirring lines in all of scripture: this Teaching is not hidden in the heavens or across the sea — it is right here, in your mouth and in your heart. He sets before them the ultimate choice — life and death, blessing and curse — and he urges them: choose life. Then Moses, now one hundred and twenty years old, tells the people he can no longer lead them, and he publicly commissions Joshua as his successor, charging him to be strong and resolute. Moses writes down the Teaching and gives it to the priests, instructing them to read it aloud before all the people every seventh year. Finally, God appears in a pillar of cloud at the Tent of Meeting, tells Moses that the people will eventually go astray, and commands him to write down a poem that will serve as a witness for future generations. Moses does so, charges the Levites to place the book of Teaching beside the Ark of the Covenant, and then begins to recite that poem before the entire congregation.

Parashah: Ki Teitzei (Deuteronomy 21:10-25:19)
Date: 2026-08-16

Welcome back to Parashah in Brief! This week we're diving into Ki Teitzei, which means "When You Go Out." This is one of the most law-packed portions in the entire Torah, found in the book of Deuteronomy, as Moses continues preparing the Israelites for life in the Promised Land.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Deuteronomy, chapter twenty-one, verse ten, through chapter twenty-five, verse nineteen. Moses opens with laws about going to war, including how to treat a captive woman — she must be given dignity and time to mourn before any marriage can take place, and she can never be sold or enslaved. From there, the portion moves into family life: a father must honor the birthright of his firstborn son even if that son's mother is unloved, and a wayward, defiant son who refuses all discipline may be brought before the town elders for judgment. Moses then lays out an extraordinary sweep of laws governing daily life — you must return a neighbor's lost animal, you must build a safety railing on your roof, and you must not plow with an ox and a donkey yoked together. There are laws protecting the vulnerable at every turn: pay your workers on time, leave behind fallen grain and unharvested olives for the stranger, the orphan, and the widow. Lenders must not humiliate borrowers — if you take someone's cloak as collateral, return it by sundown so they can sleep warm. Matters of marriage, divorce, and justice are addressed in striking detail, always with an eye toward fairness and human dignity. Merchants are commanded to use honest weights and measures, because dishonesty is an abomination before God. Moses also introduces the beautiful law of sending away the mother bird before taking her young — a small act of compassion with a great promise of long life. The portion closes with a powerful charge to remember the cruelty of Amalek, who attacked the weakest stragglers as the Israelites left Egypt, and to one day blot out that memory of ruthlessness from under heaven.

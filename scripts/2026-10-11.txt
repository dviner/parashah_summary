Parashah: Noach (Genesis 6:9-11:32)
Date: 2026-10-11

Welcome to Parashah in Brief, where each week we walk through the weekly Torah portion together. This week we're reading Parashat Noach, the second portion in the book of Genesis, which picks up right where last week's story of creation and early humanity left off — but this time, the world is about to start over.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Genesis, chapter six, verse nine, through chapter eleven, verse thirty-two. The portion opens by introducing us to Noah, a righteous and blameless man who walked with God in a generation that had become deeply corrupt and filled with lawlessness. God looks at the world and decides to bring a great flood to wipe out all living things — but God makes a covenant with Noah and instructs him to build a massive ark of wood and pitch, with three decks, and to bring his family and pairs of every living creature aboard. Noah obeys completely, and when the time comes, the fountains of the deep burst open and rain pours down for forty days and forty nights, covering even the highest mountains. Every living thing on dry land perishes — only Noah and those with him in the ark survive. After one hundred and fifty days the waters begin to recede, and the ark comes to rest on the mountains of Ararat. Noah sends out a raven and then a dove; when the dove returns with an olive leaf in its bill, Noah knows the earth is recovering. When the ground is finally dry, God tells Noah to come out and release every creature so that life can fill the earth once more. Noah builds an altar and offers sacrifices, and God, moved by the offering, promises never again to destroy the earth with a flood, setting the rainbow in the sky as an everlasting sign of that covenant. The portion then traces Noah's descendants through his three sons — Shem, Ham, and Japheth — listing the many nations that spread across the earth. It ends with the dramatic story of the Tower of Babel, where humanity, speaking one language, tries to build a tower reaching the heavens, and God scatters them across the earth and confuses their speech — bringing us right to the doorstep of Abraham's story, which begins next week.

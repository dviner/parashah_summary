Parashah: Ha'Azinu (Deuteronomy 32:1-52)
Date: 2026-09-13

Welcome to Parashah in Brief, and thanks so much for joining me this week. We're looking at Parashat Ha'Azinu, which means "Give Ear," and it's the second-to-last Torah portion in the entire Five Books of Moses. We are almost at the very end of Deuteronomy, and of Moses's life, and this portion carries the weight of that moment.

Here is our summary of Deuteronomy, chapter thirty-two, verse one, through chapter thirty-two, verse fifty-two. This portion is built almost entirely around a great poem, a song that Moses recites before all of Israel as his final testament. He begins by calling heaven and earth as witnesses, asking that his words fall on the people like rain and dew, nourishing and life-giving. He praises God as a Rock whose deeds are perfect, faithful and just in every way. Then Moses recounts how God found Israel in a barren wilderness and cared for them tenderly, like an eagle spreading its wings beneath its young, carrying them to safety. God set them in a rich land overflowing with honey, oil, milk, and the finest wheat and wine. But then the poem takes a painful turn: Israel grew prosperous and complacent, and they turned away from the God who made them, chasing after false gods and empty idols. In response, God's anger burned hot, and terrible consequences followed — famine, plague, and the sword. Yet even in wrath, God held back from total destruction, not wanting enemy nations to boast that they had defeated Israel by their own power. The song builds to a powerful climax where God declares, "See, then, that I, I am the One; there is no god beside Me. I deal death and give life; I wounded and I will heal." It ends with a promise of ultimate vindication and restoration for the people and their land. After Moses finishes reciting the song, he urges the people to take every word to heart, because this teaching is not a trifling thing — it is their very life. Then, that very same day, God tells Moses to climb Mount Nebo and look out over the Promised Land, the land he will never enter, because of the moment he broke faith with God at the waters of Meribath-kadesh. Moses will die on that mountain, just as his brother Aaron died on Mount Hor before him.

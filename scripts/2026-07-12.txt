Parashah: Devarim (Deuteronomy 1:1-3:22)
Date: 2026-07-12

Welcome to Parashah in Brief! I'm so glad you're joining me this week. We're beginning the fifth and final book of the Torah — Deuteronomy, also known in Hebrew as Devarim, meaning "Words." This portion opens Moses' great farewell address to the people of Israel as they stand on the edge of the Promised Land.

Here is our summary of Deuteronomy, chapter one, verse one, through chapter three, verse twenty-two. It is the fortieth year since the Exodus from Egypt, and Moses stands before the Israelites on the eastern side of the Jordan River in the land of Moab. Rather than simply looking forward, he looks back — recounting the journey that brought them to this moment. He recalls how God told them at Mount Horeb, also known as Sinai, that they had stayed at the mountain long enough and it was time to go claim the land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. He remembers how the burden of leading this vast nation became too heavy for him alone, so he appointed wise leaders and judges over the people, instructing them to decide disputes fairly and without favoritism. Then comes the painful memory of the spies — twelve scouts sent to survey the Promised Land who returned with beautiful fruit and a good report, yet the people lost their nerve, grumbled in their tents, and refused to go up. God, angered by their faithlessness, declared that the entire adult generation would perish in the wilderness, with the exception of Caleb and Joshua. When the people then tried to fight their way into the land on their own, against God's warning, they were chased back and defeated by the Amorites. Moses recounts the long thirty-eight years of wandering that followed, and how God instructed them to pass peacefully through the lands of Esau's descendants, the Moabites, and the Ammonites — nations whose territories were not theirs to take. Then the tide turns: God delivers King Sihon of Heshbon and King Og of Bashan into Israel's hands in dramatic victories. Moses distributes the conquered territory east of the Jordan to the tribes of Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh, on the condition that their warriors cross the Jordan to help the other tribes. Finally, Moses charges Joshua with courage, reminding him that the God who defeated these two kings will fight for Israel in every battle to come.

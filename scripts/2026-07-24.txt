Parashah: Vaetchanan (Deuteronomy 3:23-7:11)
Date: 2026-07-24

Welcome to Parashah in Brief, and thanks so much for joining me this week. We're diving into Parashat Vaetchanan, which means "and I pleaded," the second portion in the book of Deuteronomy, where Moses continues his farewell address to the Israelites as they stand on the brink of entering the Promised Land.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Deuteronomy, chapter three, verse twenty-three, through chapter seven, verse eleven. The portion opens with a deeply personal moment: Moses tells the people how he begged God to let him cross the Jordan River and see the good land, but God refused, telling him firmly, "Enough — never speak to Me of this matter again." Instead, God instructs Moses to climb to the top of Mount Pisgah, look out in every direction, and take in the land he will never enter. Moses is then told to charge Joshua with the task of leading the people forward. From there, Moses turns to the people with urgent teaching. He reminds them of the revelation at Horeb — what we also call Sinai — where they stood at the foot of a mountain blazing with fire and heard God's voice speak from the flames, yet saw no form or shape. He warns them never to make idols or worship the sun, moon, or stars, and he promises that even if they go astray, God is compassionate and will not abandon them if they return with all their heart. Then comes one of the most powerful moments in all of Torah: Moses restates the Ten Commandments — from "I am the Eternal your God" through "You shall not covet" — renewing the covenant for this new generation. After that, Moses delivers the Shema, the declaration "Hear, O Israel, the Eternal is our God, the Eternal alone," followed by the command to love God with all your heart, soul, and might, and to teach these words to your children at every moment of daily life. He urges the people to remember that they were slaves in Egypt and that God brought them out with a mighty hand, so that when they enter a land of abundance, they must not forget where they came from. The portion closes with a reminder that Israel was chosen not for its size or strength, but out of God's love and faithfulness to the promises made to their ancestors.

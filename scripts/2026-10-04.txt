Parashah: Bereshit (Genesis 1:1-6:8)
Date: 2026-10-04

Welcome back to Parashah in Brief, and what a week to be joining us — because we are starting the Torah all over again from the very beginning. This week's portion is called Bereshit, which means "In the Beginning," and it is the opening chapter of the entire Five Books of Moses.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Genesis, chapter one, verse one, through chapter six, verse eight. The portion opens with God creating the world in six days — calling forth light from darkness, separating sky from sea, filling the earth with plants, animals, and finally humankind, made in the divine image. On the seventh day, God rests and declares that day holy. Then we zoom in closer, to a garden called Eden, where God forms the first human from the dust of the earth and breathes life into his nostrils. God plants two special trees in the garden — the tree of life and the tree of knowledge of good and bad — and gives one command: do not eat from the tree of knowledge. God then creates a woman as a partner, and for a moment everything is whole and innocent. But the serpent, the shrewdest of creatures, convinces the woman that eating the forbidden fruit will open their eyes, and she eats and shares it with her husband. Immediately their innocence is shattered — they realize they are naked, they hide from God, and when confronted, they each point the finger at someone else. God pronounces consequences on all three — the serpent, the woman, and the man — and drives them out of the garden forever. Outside Eden, the first family begins, and tragedy follows quickly: Cain, consumed by jealousy, murders his brother Abel and is condemned to wander the earth. The portion then traces the generations from Adam through his son Seth all the way down to a man named Noah, cataloging lives that stretch across hundreds of years — and yet each entry ends with the same quiet words: "then he died." By the end, humanity has grown but so has corruption, until God regrets having made people at all — but one man, Noah, finds favor in God's eyes.

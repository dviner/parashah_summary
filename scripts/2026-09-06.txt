Parashah: Rosh Hashana I (Genesis 21:1-34)
Date: 2026-09-06

Welcome to Parashah in Brief, your weekly walk through the Torah portion. This week we're looking at the special reading for the first day of Rosh Hashana, the Jewish New Year, drawn from the book of Genesis. It's a deeply moving portion that takes us back to Abraham and Sarah at one of the most pivotal moments in their lives.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Genesis, chapter twenty-one, verse one, through chapter twenty-one, verse thirty-four. The portion opens with a miracle: God fulfills the long-awaited promise, and Sarah, in her old age, gives birth to a son. Abraham names the boy Isaac, and at eight days old, circumcises him as God commanded. Abraham is one hundred years old, and Sarah is overjoyed — she laughs and says that everyone who hears this news will laugh with her, marveling that she has nursed a child in her old age. The boy grows, and Abraham throws a great feast on the day Isaac is weaned. But tension quickly enters the story. Sarah sees Ishmael, the son Abraham had with Hagar the Egyptian, playing, and she demands that Abraham send Hagar and the boy away so that Ishmael will not share in Isaac's inheritance. Abraham is deeply troubled — Ishmael is his son, too — but God reassures him, telling him to listen to Sarah, for Isaac will carry on his line, and Ishmael will also become a great nation. The next morning, Abraham gives Hagar bread and a skin of water and sends her off with the child into the wilderness of Beer-sheba. When the water runs out, Hagar places the boy under a bush and sits a bowshot away, weeping because she cannot bear to watch him die. Then God hears the boy's cry and sends an angel who calls out to Hagar from heaven, telling her not to be afraid and promising that Ishmael will become a great nation. God opens her eyes and she sees a well of water, fills the skin, and gives the boy a drink. Ishmael grows up in the wilderness and becomes a skilled archer. The portion closes with a diplomatic encounter: a local king named Abimelech recognizes that God is with Abraham, and the two make a pact of loyalty at a place Abraham names Beer-sheba, meaning "well of the oath," where Abraham plants a tamarisk tree and calls upon the name of God.

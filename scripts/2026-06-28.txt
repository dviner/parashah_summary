Parashah: Pinchas (Numbers 25:10-30:1)
Date: 2026-06-28

Welcome to Parashah in Brief, where each week we walk through the Torah portion together. This week we are reading Parashat Pinchas, a rich and pivotal portion that finds the Israelites on the plains of Moab, preparing for a new chapter as they look toward the Promised Land.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Numbers, chapter twenty-five, verse ten, through chapter thirty, verse one. The portion opens with God rewarding Phinehas, the grandson of Aaron, for his zealous act that stopped a deadly plague among the Israelites. God grants Phinehas a covenant of peace and eternal priesthood for turning back divine wrath. We then learn the names of those involved in the sin — Zimri, an Israelite chieftain, and Cozbi, a Midianite woman — and God commands Moses to strike back against the Midianites for their deception. Next comes a sweeping national census, tribe by tribe, counting every man aged twenty and older who is able to bear arms, totaling just over six hundred and one thousand. Strikingly, not a single person from the original census taken at Sinai is still alive, except for Caleb and Joshua — a sobering reminder that the wilderness generation has passed away, just as God had foretold. The census also sets the stage for dividing the land of Canaan, with larger tribes receiving larger portions and smaller tribes receiving smaller ones. Then comes a remarkable moment: the five daughters of Zelophehad — Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah — step forward and boldly ask Moses for their father's share of land, since he died without sons. God affirms their claim, declaring it just, and establishes a new law of inheritance that protects the rights of daughters. The portion then turns poignant, as God tells Moses to climb the heights of Abarim and look out over the land he will never enter, because of his earlier transgression at the waters of Meribah. Moses, ever the shepherd, does not plead for himself but instead asks God to appoint a worthy successor so the people will not be like sheep without a shepherd. God chooses Joshua son of Nun, and Moses lays his hands upon him before the entire community. Finally, the portion closes with detailed instructions for the daily, Sabbath, and festival offerings — the regular rhythm of sacrifices that will sustain the people's relationship with God in their new homeland.

Parashah: Sukkot I (Leviticus 22:26-23:44)
Date: 2026-09-20

Welcome to Parashah in Brief, and a happy holiday to you! This week, in honor of Sukkot — the joyous Feast of Booths — we have a special reading drawn from the book of Leviticus. This portion lays out God's sacred calendar for the people of Israel, and it's one of the richest passages in the Torah when it comes to understanding the rhythm of Jewish life.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Leviticus, chapter twenty-two, verse twenty-six, through chapter twenty-three, verse forty-four. God speaks to Moses and begins with instructions about offerings — a newborn animal must stay with its mother for seven days before it can be brought as a sacrifice, and a thanksgiving offering must be eaten on the same day it is made. God then declares something profound: "These are My fixed times," and proceeds to lay out the entire cycle of sacred occasions for the Israelite people. First comes Shabbat, the weekly sabbath, a day of complete rest. Then comes Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month, followed immediately by the seven-day Feast of Unleavened Bread. Next, God describes the offering of the first sheaf of the harvest and the counting of fifty days to the festival of Shavuot, when new grain and leavened loaves are brought before God. Tucked into that passage is a beautiful ethical commandment: when you harvest your fields, leave the edges and the gleanings for the poor and the stranger. Moving into the seventh month, God establishes a day of rest marked by loud blasts — what we know as Rosh Hashanah. Ten days later comes the solemn Day of Atonement, Yom Kippur, a day of complete self-denial and no work whatsoever. And finally, we arrive at the heart of our holiday reading: Sukkot, the Feast of Booths, beginning on the fifteenth day of the seventh month. God commands the people to take the fruit of beautiful trees, palm branches, leafy boughs, and willows and to rejoice for seven days. They are to dwell in booths — temporary shelters — so that every generation will remember that God sheltered the Israelites when they came out of Egypt. An eighth day of solemn gathering closes out the festival season.

Parashah: Eikev (Deuteronomy 7:12-11:25)
Date: 2026-07-26

Welcome to Parashah in Brief, and thanks so much for joining me this week. We're looking at Parashat Eikev, which continues Moses's great farewell address to the Israelites as they stand on the edge of the Promised Land in the book of Deuteronomy.

Here is our summary of Deuteronomy, chapter seven, verse twelve, through chapter eleven, verse twenty-five. Moses begins with a powerful promise: if the people faithfully follow God's commandments, they will be blessed with abundant crops, healthy livestock, and freedom from the terrible diseases they witnessed in Egypt. But he also warns them not to fear the mighty nations already living in the land, reminding them how God defeated Pharaoh with signs and wonders and will do the same for them now. Moses then recalls the forty years of wandering in the wilderness, how God tested the people with hunger and then fed them with manna, that miraculous bread from heaven, to teach them that human beings do not live on bread alone but on every word that God decrees. He paints a gorgeous picture of the land they are about to enter: a land of wheat and barley, of vines and figs and pomegranates, of olive trees and honey, with streams flowing from the hills and copper waiting in the rocks. But here comes the warning that sits at the heart of this portion: when you are full and prosperous, do not grow arrogant and say, "My own power won me this wealth." Remember that it is God who gives you the strength to prosper. Moses then retells the devastating story of the golden calf, how he climbed the mountain and fasted for forty days only to come down and find the people worshipping an idol, how he shattered the tablets in anguish and threw himself before God to plead for their lives. God relented, gave Moses a second set of tablets bearing the Ten Commandments, and the journey continued. Moses closes with a soaring call: what does God ask of you? Only to revere God, to walk in God's ways, to love and serve with all your heart, and to befriend the stranger, for you yourselves were strangers in Egypt. If the people do this, the rains will come in their season, the land will flourish, and no one will stand against them.

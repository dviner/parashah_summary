Parashah: Vayera (Genesis 18:1-22:24)
Date: 2026-10-25

Welcome to Parashah in Brief! I'm so glad you're here with me this week. We're looking at Parashat Vayera, which means "and He appeared," the fourth portion in the book of Genesis. We're still deep in the story of Abraham and Sarah, and this week's portion is packed with drama, testing, and divine promise.

<break time="1.5s" />

Here is our summary of Genesis, chapter eighteen, verse one, through chapter twenty-two, verse twenty-four. The portion opens with Abraham sitting at the entrance of his tent in the heat of the day when he looks up and sees three mysterious visitors approaching. He rushes to welcome them with extraordinary hospitality — preparing a lavish meal of cakes, curds, milk, and a tender calf. One of the visitors delivers an astonishing promise: by this time next year, Sarah will have a son. Sarah, listening from inside the tent, laughs to herself — she and Abraham are both very old — but God asks Abraham, "Is anything too wondrous for God?" The visitors then turn their attention toward the city of Sodom, and God reveals to Abraham that its wickedness has grown so great that destruction is coming. Abraham boldly bargains with God, asking if God will spare the city for the sake of fifty innocent people, then forty-five, then forty, thirty, twenty, and finally ten — but even ten righteous souls cannot be found. Two angels arrive in Sodom and are taken in by Abraham's nephew Lot, but the townspeople surround the house with violent intentions, confirming the city's corruption. The angels rescue Lot and his family, urging them to flee without looking back, but Lot's wife turns to look and is transformed into a pillar of salt. Fire and sulfur rain down, and Sodom and Gomorrah are completely destroyed. After this devastation, the long-awaited promise is fulfilled — Sarah gives birth to Isaac, and Abraham circumcises him on the eighth day. But tension arises in the household: Sarah demands that Hagar and her son Ishmael be sent away, and God tells Abraham to listen to Sarah, promising that Ishmael too will become a great nation. Then comes the ultimate test — God commands Abraham to take Isaac, his beloved son, to the land of Moriah and offer him as a burnt offering, setting the stage for one of the most powerful scenes in all of scripture.
